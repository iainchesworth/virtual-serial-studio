**The Windows virtual COM port driver follows the serial rules of a real port.** A read waits for data under the `SetCommTimeouts` rules, including the interval, total, "return at once" and "return on the first byte" combinations. `WaitCommEvent` completes when a masked event happens and keeps events that happened before the wait, a new `SetCommMask` releases a waiting call, and `PurgeComm`, `CancelIoEx` and closing the handle end a waiting read without hanging. DTR and RTS come back as DSR, carrier detect and CTS and raise their events, the port reports its properties, and it goes back to quiet when the handle closes. Guest scripts in `drivers/windows/test` install the driver and run the seven checks D1 to D7 through the Win32 serial API. The driver has not been loaded in a guest yet, so `vss::driver_available()` still reports no driver.
